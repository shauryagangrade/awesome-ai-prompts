# Reusable prompt: literature review

Copy-paste the block below into any AI coding agent to survey the research on
a topic using primary sources, then synthesize what is actually known.

Keywords: literature review, research papers, sources, citations, synthesis, gaps

---

Review the literature on [topic]. Build the review from primary sources and
synthesize what is established, what is contested, and what is unknown.

## Steps

1. **Scope** - State the question, the fields and years in scope, and the
   inclusion criteria (what counts as a source and what does not).
2. **Find** - Locate candidate papers from primary venues and citation chains.
   Prefer the publisher or preprint record over a blog summary. Keep a list of
   every source with title, authors, year, venue, and a stable link or DOI.
3. **Verify** - For each source you cite, open it and confirm the claim against
   the paper itself, not an abstract or a secondary summary. Record the
   page or section that supports each key claim. Drop anything you cannot
   verify.
4. **Synthesize** - Group the findings by theme. State where sources agree,
   where they conflict, and how strong the evidence is. Do not average away
   disagreements.
5. **Report** - Write the review with inline citations to the sources you
   verified, plus a short list of open questions and gaps.

## Verification

- [ ] Scope and inclusion criteria are stated before sources are gathered.
- [ ] Every cited claim points to a primary source you actually opened.
- [ ] Conflicting findings are presented as conflicts, not smoothed over.
- [ ] Claims you could not verify were dropped, not included with a hedge.
- [ ] The output names gaps and open questions explicitly.

## Rules

- Never cite a paper you did not open; an abstract is not a source.
- Never present a single study as consensus, or a consensus as proof.
- If the topic is too broad to cover honestly, say so and propose a narrower
  question before writing the review.
